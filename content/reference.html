7. Establish relationships

Print/cut the NPC portraits.

  1. GM divides portraits among players.
  2. Each player removes 1 portrait; pass remaining cards to neighbours and repeat until each player holds 2.
  3. GM shuffles those portraits and deals them again.
  4. Each player writes 1 trait for each portrait.
  5. Pass 1 portrait left and 1 right; receiving players add a second trait.
  6. Optional: shuffle the trait post-its and randomly reassign them among portraits.
  7. Put portraits in the centre. For each: define name, occupation, short history, psychological traits, and why characters may know them.
  8. Assign 1–3 specific services.

Start with 5 relationship points. Spend to raise relationships: Contact 1 · Friend 2 · Intimate 3.

8. Choose skills and abilities

Choose 5 skills and 2 abilities. Any of these choices may be replaced with a cybernetic augmentation; total augmentations cannot exceed CON.

Use Character Options and Augmentations.

10. Character questions

After creation, each player asks another player 1 question about that character. Continue until every character has answered ≥1. Asked player may refuse, redirect, or alter the question.

Regular Play

Golden Rule

Important question whose answer matters to the adventure but the GM does not know or want to decide → 1d6: 4+ yes · 1–3 no.

Checks

3d6 + Attribute + bonuses; 1 d6 is coloured. Success → total ≥ difficulty or opposition. Opposed check: opponent rolls 3d6 + relevant value; NPC opposition uses Power.

Difficulty Target
Simple 9
Tricky 12
Difficult 15
Very hard 18
Extremely complicated 21
Almost impossible 24

Triple 6 → automatic success +1 Luck. Triple 1 → automatic failure +1 Luck + complications. No obstacle or meaningful consequence → automatic success. Impossible task → automatic failure. Success by about 5–6+ → extra benefit appropriate to action. Failure may become success at a cost; player may reject cost and accept failure. Failure may instead create a new obstacle/complication.

Cooperation

Help another character → +1 to their roll. Whole-group check → group succeeds if ≥half succeed individually.

Saves

Reaction to sudden danger: matching Attribute + applicable skill. Failure → danger applies. Success → danger cancelled or reduced as situation permits.

HP, wounds, care

Full night’s sleep → restore all HP + Connection.

0 HP → 1 wound + unconscious until care, rest, or 1 Luck restores consciousness. Each wound → maximum HP −5. Maximum HP reduced to 0 by wounds → death unless suitable new augmentation replaces damaged body; installing it clears all current wounds. Clinic → remove 1 wound for ¥4.

Care
1/day/character: INT + applicable skill vs 15 → restore HP = coloured die. Connection spent on roll → +2 HP/point. ¥ spent → +5 HP/¥.

Connection

At 0 Connection → relationships cannot activate. Matrix exception: programs still launch, but each automatically triggers alarm.

Plans

Before a mission, each character may pay ¥2 → regain Connection equal to 1 chosen Attribute rank.

Preparation domain: STR/CON — heavy work, vehicle modification, demolition gear; DEX/INT — data/electronics; WIS/CHA — information/contacts. Preparation need not be listed in advance.

During that mission, spend Connection, including pooled Connection, to establish retroactive preparation when an obstacle/problem appears.

Connection Effect
1 bypass minor obstacle or save time on something otherwise easy
3 escape important danger or bypass obstacle requiring prepared material, skill, or information
5+ bypass major obstacle or immediate lethal danger, substantially changing established situation

Face may use normal Connection as plan points without pre-mission preparation.

Cycle

Players decide when cycle changes; a cycle may represent hours to weeks. Character sheet gives recovery/XP effects. Cycle change also advances opposing factions’ plans.

Combat turn

Turn = 1 action + 1 manoeuvre.

Actions
attack, skill, program.
Manoeuvres
move, take cover, aim, charge, help.

Supporting manoeuvre → action roll +1. Action may be spent as manoeuvre; manoeuvre cannot be spent as action.

Exact movement: STR or DEX roll → metres moved this turn.

Initiative acts high → low. Surprise → surprised side’s opponents +1d6 Initiative.

The character sheet gives standard attack/damage formulas and the Feat/Risk tables. Each combat turn, choose 1 Feat; GM rolls 1 Risk. If that Risk directly opposes the chosen Feat, ignore the Risk and apply the Feat.

On hit, coloured damage die explodes: 6 → roll/add another d6; repeat on each 6. Damage → target HP. Damage may be split among one or more opponents whose Defence ≤ primary target Defence.

Combat stunts

Describe intended result/tactical goal. GM chooses Attribute attack vs Defence, check, save, or description.

Attack Attributes may include: STR force; DEX precision; CHA feint/distraction; INT setup/tactics.

Damage may represent effect strength instead of HP. GM may convert damage to save difficulty; source example: 7 + damage.

Scene details

When acting, players may establish plausible previously unspecified terrain/details: cover, hiding place, choke point, similar feature. Established detail becomes true for everyone.

Social resolution

Minor social action → 1 quick roll, or none if uncertainty is unnecessary. Information exchange → play conversation directly; spoken content determines what is revealed. Social conflict where speech + mechanics both matter → CHA with situational modifiers, or multiple rolls/rounds if needed. No fixed social-combat subsystem.

Matrix Rules

All characters can use nexus and programs.

Program activation

Program cost = program level in Connection unless stated; use = 1 action. Programs normally manipulate Matrix or connected objects live/on site. Matrix research, custom coding, sorting/searching information may use normal skills.

At 0 Connection → programs may still launch, but each automatically triggers alarm.

Firewalls

Pay program cost → roll 1d6. Roll ≤ firewall → blocked; cost spent. Stop, pay/retry, or force through → program works + alarm.

Firewall System
0 unprotected nexus/machine
1–2 most automated systems/consumer nexuses
3–4 common corporate nexuses/systems
5 strongest generally available corporate/security systems
6 corporate anti-zone systems; described as impossible to hack

Alarms

Forced firewall passage → 1d6 alarm. Local security may add a modifier, usually half local security level. Abilities/equipment may reduce the roll.

Roll Effect
1 no effective response
2 all Connection costs +1 until cycle end
3 Matrix unavailable to you until scene end
4 Matrix unavailable until cycle end unless connection identity changes; remaining nearby brings response
5 opposing programs spot you; GM rolls 1d6 = Connection still usable before full identification; tracing follows
6 corporate forces arrive in 1d2 minutes; opposing programs mark/track you
7+ counter-intrusion program attacks directly

Vehicles

CPA operation

A CPA works like being on foot: fight, run, jump, and act normally; its size/mass may grant circumstantial advantages or disadvantages. Base CPA: Defence +2, all attack damage +5, 3 wounds. Unarmed + unarmoured CPA = legal civilian/work vehicle; combat CPA = security-restricted.

Speed and chases

Very Slow < Slow < Average < Fast < Ultra-Fast.

Chase → usually compare relative speed. Terrain, traffic, road conditions, vehicle size may change result.

Platform

Vehicles may carry useful tools/equipment such as sensors, weapons, manipulators. Capacity follows vehicle size; heavy load may reduce speed by 1 step; otherwise use common sense.

Wounds and collisions

Vehicle wound capacity = 1–5; character vehicles generally ≥3. Qualifying hit/collision → wounds as specified. At wound limit → out of service until repaired or destroyed, depending on fiction.

Collision wounds by current speed: Slow 1 · Average 2 · Fast 3 · Ultra-Fast 4. Frontal +1; rear −1.

Armour

Light 1d6 any firearm

Heavy 3d6 heavy cannon, rockets, missiles

Defence and damage

Stationary Defence 7. Moving Defence 11. Ultra-Fast +2 Defence. Target passenger → +4 Defence + passenger protection bonus.

Successful hit with sufficient calibre → 1 vehicle wound. Do not roll normal HP damage against vehicle.

Driving

Use DEX or INT; sometimes CON for endurance. Roll only when avoiding obstacle/problem.

Normal driver in combat → 1 action/turn; no additional manoeuvre; no bonus action from Initiative.

Neural interface

Neuronic → interface directly with vehicle/machine; piloting +2; access interfaced equipment.

Rigger Transfer

While interfaced → act as vehicle; 1 action + 1 manoeuvre/turn; receive Initiative bonus actions; vehicle HP = Rigger personal HP; vehicle wound capacity +1; hits remove vehicle HP before wounds; if aboard, Rigger may take wounds instead of vehicle.

Campaign Procedures

Panopticom

At cycle change, when cell is ready, submit report. Accepted report → each character +¥5 +100 XP.

Track 1 criteria sheet per investigation:

  1. issue and angle;
  2. basic facts: who, what, where, when, how, why;
  3. causes and consequences;
  4. deeper facts and ramifications;
  5. impact on society;
  6. evidence;
  7. images;
  8. put the findings to those involved and seek their response;
  9. involvement of The Other;
  10. ethics — GM checks sincerity/professionalism from the characters’ point of view; being honestly deceived need not fail this criterion.
Submission
count fulfilled criteria → GM rolls 2d6 modified by cell reputation. Result > fulfilled criteria → rejected. Rejected report may be resubmitted after ≥1 additional criterion is fulfilled.
Reputation
range −3…+3. Each broadcast may change it ±1, including later reassessment. Apply reputation to report-acceptance roll and ¥ payout. Source does not specify the arithmetic for modifying ¥ payout.
